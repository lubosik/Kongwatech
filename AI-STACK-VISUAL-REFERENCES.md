## 1. Creator Ops Hub — Resources

URL: <https://creatoropshub.com/resources/>

Screenshots:

- Desktop: `docs/evidence/ai-stack-visual-references/creator-ops-desktop.png`
- Mobile 390px: `docs/evidence/ai-stack-visual-references/creator-ops-mobile-390.png`

Worth borrowing:

1. The page opens with “Tools I Actually Use,” immediately establishing personal experience rather than generic comparison content.
2. The intro and disclosure both use plain first-person language and explain why a recommendation earned its place.
3. Categories and individual tools flow in a predictable vertical order, so a visitor always knows what comes next.

Do not borrow:

1. The full rainbow-gradient hero is visually loud and works against Kongwa’s requested white, calm recommendation-page direction.
2. The affiliate message is effectively repeated twice before the first tool, delaying value on mobile.
3. The long table of contents adds scanning overhead that is unnecessary for a nine-tool shortlist.

# One-page design synthesis

## The shared conversion pattern

The useful references converge on the same sequence:

1. **Personal premise.** The creator says why this list exists and makes it clear these are tools they personally use or trust.
2. **Human disclosure.** Affiliate status is disclosed near the recommendations in plain language, without turning the hero into legal copy.
3. **One recommendation at a time.** A recognisable logo/name is followed by a short explanation grounded in the creator’s actual work.
4. **Proof only where it helps.** A video, screenshot or short outcome is included when it makes the recommendation easier to believe or understand—not as decoration.
5. **One explicit action.** The button says what will happen: “Try…”, “Visit…” or “Explore…”. It is never hidden behind abstract product language.

## Direction for Kongwa

The rebuild should be mobile-first and feel like Lubosi answering a repeated question from a follower. Use a white or gently warm-white page, a narrow editorial measure, near-black type, restrained amber accents and subtle horizontal dividers. The global header can remain, but the page hero should be compact enough that the opening trust statement and disclosure are understood within roughly the first one to one-and-a-half mobile viewports. Instantly should begin immediately afterwards.

Every tool should reuse one simple section grammar: small product identity and affiliate label, tool heading, “Here’s what I use it for” or a similarly natural lead-in, no more than a few short first-person paragraphs, optional proof, then a large high-contrast CTA. On mobile this is always one column; buttons should be nearly full-width and at least 48px high. Desktop may place genuine media alongside copy for Higgsfield, but it should not introduce a second design system.

Trust should come from specificity. “I use this to find leads, verify email addresses and run campaigns” is stronger than a vendor feature list. “If you’ve seen my realistic AI UGC…” is stronger than a cinematic product slogan. Active affiliate sections need a small visible “Affiliate link” label; non-affiliate tools must not receive one. The full disclosure can repeat once in the footer.

## Visual guardrails

- No dark full-screen sections, dashboard shells, metrics, filters, system diagrams or directory metadata.
- No giant hero illustration, background video, animated carousels or modal interruption.
- No nested cards. Most sections should live directly on the page and be separated by whitespace and a fine neutral rule.
- Do not repeat the disclosure or surround it with warning styling; a quiet warm-neutral box is enough.
- Use gold/amber as a small brand cue, not as a border around every section.
- Do not let categories or jump links become the primary interaction. Nine tools are short enough to scroll.
- Preserve a consistent rhythm across all nine tools; media earns a place only when it increases comprehension or trust.

The intended result is simpler than any one reference: Ali Abdaal’s calm light presentation, Creator Ops Hub’s first-person honesty, George Blackman’s CTA clarity, and Future Tools’ curation discipline—without their oversized hero art, lead magnets, dark database chrome or intrusive overlays.
